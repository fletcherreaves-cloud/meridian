### 2nd Side Formula  ·  _QSRSoft® Reports_  ([open](https://support.qsrsoft.com/hc/en-us/articles/360006602253-2nd-Side-Formula))

Question What is the 2 nd side formula? Answer Healthy Usage Healthy Usage is a check to see if both sides are being used once a certain number of items come through. It starts with checking if there have been 36/38 (breakfast/regular menu) items in a quarter hour period. Once that threshold is met, then at least 20% of the items need to be on each side to count as Healthy Usage. The :15 result will be either 0% or 100% (if threshold met) If the threshold is met for a full hour but only 3 of the 4 quarter hours meet 20% on each side, the hour would show as 75%. The same averaging would calculate for the full day information. If a quarter hour does not meet 36/38 total items sold, it will display as N/A. For example: If 38 items were sold in a quarter hour, each side would need to prep 8 items in order to get 100%.(38 * 0.2 = 7.6 rounded up to 8) If both sides do not prep at least 8 items, it would show 0%. For the full day average, it only counts the 0% and 100% quarter hours since the N/A quarter hours did not have enough items to meet the threshold. Added info: Just a quick recap for future reference - if side 2 is open when it does not need to be, or not open when it should be, it will lower your percentage. A good ratio when it should be open and operational is 1:1 or 2:1. If you open the 2nd side at least once we determine that you have a 2nd side. An "N/A" in this field indicates that there were no quarter hours during the daypart shown when the 2nd side of the Prep Line was required to be open

### What is Causing My Food Over Base Unexplained Difference  ·  _QSRSoft® Inventory_  ([open](https://support.qsrsoft.com/hc/en-us/articles/4420169221143-What-is-Causing-My-Food-Over-Base-Unexplained-Difference))

Actual Dollars On the right side of the Food Over Base (FOB) page are the “actual dollars” that have been used in calculating food cost for the restaurant. On the left side of the FOB page are the “calculated dollars” that have been used in calculating food cost in the restaurant. The “dollar difference” between these two is added back to the calculated side to make them equal. Note: The underlined words on the Food Over Base page are hyperlinks that provide details about how the numbers were derived. Here are the common issues that cause the difference between the “actual dollars” and the “calculated dollars”. These are not in a sequence of impact, as any one of these by itself can have a greater impact depending on the cost of the WRIN or the quantity involved. Recipe errors If the recipe for an item is not accurate it can impact both the base food cost and stat loss amounts, which then creates unexplained difference. Case price change over the month If a restaurant isn’t buying a consistent amount and the price is going up (or down), then ending inventory is influenced by the final invoice price. However, the restaurant may not have purchased much of that item with the price difference, in relationship to the total amount of inventory. Negative actual usage on the stat variance report If the restaurant ends with more than they started, the system can’t calculate a stat loss. Therefore, that unknown loss will be in unexplained based on how much was purchased. Using inactive items in an active recipe (See Inventory Analysis Report #6) This is the dreaded bulk ketchup and others. The restaurant needs to remove keys from the POS if they no longer have the products as using those keys creates unexplained. Inaccurate transfer quantities in or out Because a transfer is an exchange of “dollars”, an inaccurate transfer can create both a stat loss or gain and a matching difference in unexplained. Not inventorying “non-recipe” items Sauces are a good example. If there is a promotion for an item that provides sauce, more sauce may have been purchased. If this sauce is never inventoried, the calculated side of the Food Over Base page is not aware of this higher usage. However, the purchase dollars are higher than the prior average amount used. Not inventorying “recipe” items These are mostly ones where the crew control the portion size. Fries are a good example. If the crew is giving out more than the selected size, but the managers are not inventorying, then some of the loss dollars will be in unexplained until an inventory is taken, then it will show in stat loss. High B-Red or T-Red The “Before or After Total Reduction of Items” from the POS can indicate the crew is pushing many more buttons that what is really going out to the customers. No matter if it is theft or not, incorrectly recording the order creates differences. While this mostly shows in stat loss, per the cases above, it can also impact unexplained difference.

### What Makes Up Food Promotions on My Food Over Base Page  ·  _QSRSoft® Inventory_  ([open](https://support.qsrsoft.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037193034-What-Makes-Up-Food-Promotions-on-My-Food-Over-Base-Page))
