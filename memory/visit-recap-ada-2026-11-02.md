## Strengths identified

- Leadership engagement — GMs actively participated, shared real restaurant experience.
- French fry quality — several restaurants improved hot/fresh/crispy via better cook rotation +
  fryer management.
- Quality awareness — leaders aware of product temp, sandwich presentation, order accuracy as
  customer-facing concerns.
- Equipment maintenance — some strong maintenance routines / reliability improvements reported.
- Collaborative learning — open best-practice sharing, hands-on demos.
- Continuous improvement — active work already underway on meat quality, fryer maintenance,
  product freshness, UHC management.

## Opportunities identified (full list)

- Inconsistent prep quantities; overfilled prep-table containers.
- Excessive bun preparation; gaps in product rotation/tempering.
- Inconsistent grill cleaning, scraping, calibration verification.
- UHC tray rotation / holding-time adherence gaps.
- Inconsistent product storage, secondary time labeling, packaging practices.
- Crew gaps in fryer filtration + frozen product handling understanding.
- Inconsistent leadership travel paths / verification of procedures.
- Need to strengthen hands-on training, coaching, accountability.

Corporate's own framing: these are **execution-consistency gaps, not a lack of available
procedures** — crew/leadership mostly know the right answer; the gap is in doing it the same way
every time and someone checking that they did.

## Selected focus areas (org's own 3 priorities)

1. **Prep Table**
2. **Buns**
3. **Grill Procedures**

## Root cause analysis (corporate's own 5 causes)

1. Inconsistent training/procedural understanding — crew learning by observing peers rather than
   standardized training.
2. Lack of consistent management follow-up — expectations communicated but not consistently
   verified; incorrect practices persist when not checked via daily leadership routines.
3. Prioritizing speed over quality — rushing to hit service times drives over-prep, inconsistent
   presentation, skipped quality steps.
4. Inconsistent prep/rotation — over-preparation + rotation misunderstanding → waste + quality
   variance.
5. Equipment maintenance routine gaps — inconsistent grill scraping/rapid cleaning/calibration
   verification → cooking inconsistency.

**Overall root cause (corporate's synthesis):** the gap is in the link between training,
execution, and leadership follow-up/verification — not a knowledge gap at the leadership level.

## Follow-up expectation (corporate's own ask)

Management to communicate the 3 goals to teams, set clear expectations, assign accountability,
and **incorporate verification into daily shift-leadership routines**. Progress reviewed at a
follow-up to check for sustained improvement, not just a one-time fix.

**Final takeaway (verbatim):** *"The greatest opportunity is not simply identifying what needs
to improve, but ensuring that proper training, consistent execution, and management follow-up
become part of everyday restaurant operations."*
